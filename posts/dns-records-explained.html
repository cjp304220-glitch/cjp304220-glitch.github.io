DNS Records Explained: A, CNAME, TXT, MX Without the Jargon

DNS is the internet's phone book, and almost every "my site doesn't open" moment is a DNS mistake: wrong record type, wrong name, or impatience with caching. Fifteen minutes here saves hours later.

The records you will actually touch

A / AAAA

Points a name at an IP address. A = IPv4 (203.0.113.10), AAAA = IPv6. Typical setup: @ (the bare domain) → A → your server IP.

CNAME

Points a name at another name. www → yourdomain.com. means "whatever yourdomain resolves to, use that too". Handy because the IP can change in one place. Caveat: most registrars refuse a CNAME on the apex (@) because it breaks mail and verification lookups — use an A record, or flattening where your DNS host offers it (Cloudflare flattens apex CNAMEs automatically).

TXT

Free-form text, used for proof of ownership (Google/Bing site verification), email anti-spam (SPF/DKIM), and other services saying "yes, this domain belongs to this account". You will add TXT records any time a service asks you to "verify your domain".

MX

Where email goes. Each MX has a priority number — lower wins. Get these right before pointing a domain you rely on for mail.

NS

Delegates the whole domain (or subdomain) to a set of nameservers. Changing NS is how you move a domain from your registrar's default DNS to Cloudflare, and it is the slowest change to take effect.

Name conventions

@        the bare domain (example.com)
www      the www subdomain
*        wildcard: matches anything else
mail     a custom name: mail.example.com

Propagation is really just caching

There is no global "propagation switch". Every resolver caches answers for the record's TTL (time to live) — from seconds to a day. Lower TTL to 300 before a planned change, make the change, raise it afterwards. Digging around the world takes minutes; waiting out someone's long TTL takes hours.

Check what the world sees

dig A example.com            # the A record, as your resolver sees it
dig +short CNAME www.example.com
dig NS example.com           # who serves DNS
nslookup -type=txt example.com
curl -sI https://example.com | head -5    # does the site answer?

If the panel says one thing and dig says another, the panel is right and your resolver has an old copy — try another network or wait out the TTL.

Common setups, end to end

  • Site on Cloudflare Pages: no A record needed — set NS to Cloudflare and add the apex + www as CNAMEs pointing at yourproject.pages.dev (Cloudflare flattens the apex). Full walkthrough: point a domain to Cloudflare Pages.
  • Server with a fixed IP: apex A record → IP, www CNAME → apex, MX only if you run mail.
  • Verification TXT: copy the exact value the service gives you; these are case-sensitive.

That's the whole mental model: names map to IPs (A/AAAA) or other names (CNAME), text proves ownership (TXT), mail has its own lane (MX), and everything is cached for its TTL. Once that lands, the platform comparisons like GitHub Pages vs Cloudflare Pages stop being abstract.
