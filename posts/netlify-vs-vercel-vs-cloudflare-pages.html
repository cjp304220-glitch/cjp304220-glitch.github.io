Netlify vs Vercel vs Cloudflare Pages (2026): Which Free Tier Actually Lasts?

By Naresh · October 24, 2026 · Hosting & Web

On this page

  • What each one is genuinely best at

Netlify, Vercel and Cloudflare Pages all let you deploy for free, with HTTPS and preview links. The difference is how they cap you — and 2026 was the year the caps moved. Here is where things stand, checked against official pricing pages in October 2026.

Netlify switched to credits in September 2025 and retuned them in April 2026 (bandwidth now costs double what it did); Vercel's Hobby plan still requires non-commercial use. Free tiers shift often — check current pricing before you build a business on one.

What each one is genuinely best at

  • Cloudflare Pages: blogs, docs, portfolios, anything static with real traffic — unlimited bandwidth means a viral day costs nothing. Direct CLI upload works without a Git repo.
  • Vercel: Next.js and React apps — the framework and the platform are built together, so builds are fast and config is zero. Hobby is for learning and personal projects; selling on it needs Pro ($20/user/month, plus usage credit).
  • Netlify: the polished workflow: forms, functions and edge handlers in one dashboard, generous free previews. Watch the credits — 15 per production deploy means a deploy-heavy month burns them, and hitting zero pauses everything on the account.

Pick with one question

"Will this site earn money?"

  • No (learning, portfolio, experiment): any of the three — start where your framework feels native
  • Yes, static content: Cloudflare Pages — no commercial restrictions, no bandwidth meter
  • Yes, with a Next.js app: Vercel Pro from day one, or Cloudflare's Next.js support on Workers

The honest summary: Vercel wins on developer experience, Netlify on built-in features, Cloudflare on limits that never surprise you. For a static blog like this one, the unlimited-bandwidth answer is why this site runs on Cloudflare Pages — and GitHub Pages remains the other zero-cost mirror option.
