How to Choose a Monitor for Programming (Without Wasting Money)

By Naresh · October 24, 2026 · Gadgets & Setup

On this page
  • The specs that matter
  • Text clarity: the underrated one
  • Connections and desk logic
  • The order I would buy in

Monitor marketing is built for gamers: 240 Hz, RGB, curved ultrawides. Writing code needs almost none of that — it needs sharp text, comfortable brightness and a stand that puts the panel at eye level. Here is the decision in the order it should be made.

The specs that matter

SpecWhat to getWhy
Resolution27" at 1440p (or 24" at 1080p minimum)Text sharpness; 1440p fits an editor + terminal + docs side by side
PanelIPSStable colors and viewing angles; VA smears scrolling text, OLED risks burn-in with static panels and costs more
Refresh60–75 Hz is fineCoding does not benefit from high refresh; save the money
CoatingMatteReflections under office lights get old fast
StandHeight + tilt adjust (or VESA)Screen top at eye level saves your neck more than any panel upgrade

Prices swing by region and season — check current pricing on your preferred sizes rather than trusting any list's numbers.

Text clarity: the underrated one

  • Higher pixel density = sharper glyphs. 27" 1440p (~109 PPI) is the comfort sweet spot; 4K at 27" looks cleaner but needs scaling, which older apps render badly
  • On Windows, run ClearType Text Tuner after setup; on macOS, keep default "Looks like" scaling
  • Prefer fonts designed for screens in your editor (mono fonts with clear l/I/1)

Connections and desk logic

  • USB-C with power delivery is the single cable that charges a laptop and carries the picture — worth prioritizing if you dock a laptop daily
  • Two 27" monitors vs one ultrawide: dual monitors give you two full-screen windows without losing anything to the curve; ultrawides win for wide diffs and video timelines. For code, most people are happy either way — pick based on your desk, not the internet
  • Check the port count against your machine before buying — dongles are forever

The order I would buy in

  1. 27" 1440p IPS with an adjustable stand
  2. Second monitor (same model keeps scaling consistent)
  3. Monitor light bar for night work
  4. Only then: the fancy stuff — high refresh, ultrawide, color-critical panels

A monitor sits in the middle of every work hour you have — it is usually a better buy than a faster keyboard. For the rest of the desk, see Home Coding Setup Essentials, and for the input side, choosing a keyboard for programming.
